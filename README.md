# Wind Farm Layout Optimization using Gurobi

## Shell.ai Hackathon for Sustainable and Affordable Energy -- 2020

## 1. Introduction

Wind turbine placement strongly affects a wind farm's energy production.
An upstream turbine extracts energy from the incoming wind and creates a
downstream wake region with reduced wind speed. Downstream turbines
inside this region consequently generate less power.

This project investigates wind farm layout optimization using
mathematical optimization and Gurobi. It replaces the Genetic Algorithm
approach described in the original README with a Gurobi-based
formulation for turbine-coordinate optimization.

## 2. Problem Statement

The original Shell.ai Hackathon challenge asks for the placement of 50
wind turbines in a hypothetical offshore wind farm to maximize Annual
Energy Production (AEP).

  Parameter                            Value
  ---------------------------- -------------
  Number of turbines                      50
  Farm dimensions                4 km × 4 km
  Rotor diameter                       100 m
  Hub height                           100 m
  Minimum boundary clearance            50 m
  Minimum turbine separation           400 m

### Constraints

**Perimeter constraint**

For turbine (i), with coordinates ((x_i,y_i)):

\[ 50 `\leq `{=tex}x_i,y_i `\leq 3950`{=tex} \]

**Proximity constraint**

Every pair of turbines must be separated by at least 400 m:

\[ (x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2
`\geq 400`{=tex}\^2,`\quad `{=tex}i`\ne `{=tex}j \]

## 3. Wake Model and Energy Production

### 3.1 Jensen/PARK wake model

The project uses the reference wake and power calculations supplied
through the Shell.ai AEP calculator. The wake effect is represented
using the Jensen/PARK model.

The wake expansion is:

\[ R(x)=R+k_w x \]

where (R) is the rotor radius, (x) is the downstream distance, and (k_w)
is the wake expansion coefficient.

The individual wake deficit is:

\[ `\delta`{=tex}\_{ij} = (1-`\sqrt{1-C_T}`{=tex})
`\left`{=tex}(`\frac{R}{R+k_w x_{ij}}`{=tex}`\right`{=tex})\^2 \]

where (C_T) is the thrust coefficient.

### 3.2 Combined wake effect

Multiple wake deficits are combined using the root-sum-square (RSS)
method:

\[ `\delta`{=tex}\_i\^{eff} = `\sqrt{\sum_j \delta_{ij}^{2}}`{=tex} \]

The effective wind speed at turbine (i) is:

\[ V_i^{eff}=V^{free}(1-`\delta`{=tex}\_i\^{eff}) \]

Turbine power is obtained from the reference power curve using the
effective wind speed.

### 3.3 Annual Energy Production

The objective is to maximize expected annual energy production across
wind scenarios:

\[ AEP = `\frac{8760}{1000}`{=tex} `\sum`{=tex}\_s p_s `\sum`{=tex}*i
P*{i,s} \]

Here, (p_s) is the probability of scenario (s), and (P\_{i,s}) is the
power generated by turbine (i) in that scenario. The factor 8760
represents the hours in a year.

The full wind-resource formulation considers 36 wind directions and 15
wind-speed intervals, giving 540 scenarios.

## 4. Gurobi Optimization Approach

The decision variables are the turbine coordinates:

\[ (x_i,y_i),`\quad `{=tex}i=1,`\ldots`{=tex},N \]

The formulation incorporates:

1.  Turbine coordinate variables.
2.  Farm boundary constraints.
3.  Minimum inter-turbine separation constraints.
4.  Wind-direction-dependent wake interactions.
5.  Jensen/PARK wake deficit calculations.
6.  Combined wake deficit using RSS.
7.  Turbine power production using piecewise-linear approximations.
8.  Expected AEP maximization across wind scenarios.

The model uses Gurobi optimization capabilities, including
piecewise-linear approximations and general constraints, to represent
relationships required by the formulation.

### Role of the Shell.ai reference implementation

Shell.ai provided the reference AEP calculator and wake/power
calculations. Our contribution is to incorporate the corresponding
relationships into a Gurobi mathematical optimization formulation for
turbine placement. The reference wake model and AEP calculator are not
claimed as original work by this project.

## 5. Experiments and Results

Because of the computational complexity of the problem, smaller
configurations were investigated to test the formulation.

### Experiment 1: 10 turbines -- Single wind scenario

Configuration: - 10 turbines. - Wind speed: 15 m/s. - Wind direction:
360°. - Minimum turbine separation: 400 m.

  Metric                                                     Result
  ------------------------------------------- ---------------------
  Initial objective                                     29.85206 MW
  Gurobi objective                                      29.95663 MW
  Independent reference-equation validation             29.95704 MW
  Minimum actual separation                              400.0156 m
  Relative improvement                          Approximately 0.35%

The layout satisfies the boundary and minimum-separation constraints.
The independent reference-equation calculation is close to the Gurobi
piecewise-linear objective.

### Experiment 2: 5 turbines -- Full 540 wind scenarios

Configuration: - 5 turbines. - 36 wind directions. - 15 wind speeds. -
540 scenarios. - Scenario probabilities derived from the 2007
wind-resource data.

  Metric                                 Result
  ---------------------------- ----------------
  Best feasible AEP found          56.89438 GWh
  Best objective bound             57.44670 GWh
  MIP gap                               0.9708%
  Runtime                        423.55 seconds
  Minimum turbine separation              800 m

**Returned turbine coordinates**

  Turbine     X-coordinate (m)   Y-coordinate (m)
  --------- ------------------ ------------------
  T1                       300                300
  T2                      1100                300
  T3                      1900                300
  T4                      2700                300
  T5                      3500                300

The returned solution retained the initial equally spaced layout. Gurobi
terminated within the configured 1% MIP gap but did not find an improved
feasible layout during this run.

> **Important:** This is a five-turbine adaptation of the full-scenario
> formulation, not the official 50-turbine competition result. The
> supplied reference evaluator has a 50-turbine requirement.

## 9. Limitations and Future Work

1.  **Computational complexity:** Wake interactions and
    scenario-dependent constraints increase substantially with the
    number of turbines and scenarios.
2.  **Nonlinear relationships:** Wake propagation, combined wake
    effects, and turbine power production introduce nonlinear
    relationships.
3.  **Piecewise-linear approximation:** Approximation can cause small
    differences between the optimization objective and the reference AEP
    calculation.
4.  **Full-scale optimization:** A computationally effective formulation
    is still required to optimize all 50 turbines across all 540
    scenarios.
5.  **Improved formulation:** Better initial layouts, tighter
    formulations, and decomposition strategies may improve solution
    quality and computational performance.

Future work can investigate stronger mathematical formulations and
decomposition-based approaches to improve scalability.

## 10. Conclusion

This project investigates wind farm layout optimization using
mathematical optimization with Gurobi. It incorporates the reference
wake and power relationships supplied by Shell.ai into a formulation
involving turbine coordinates, boundary restrictions, minimum
separation, and wind-dependent wake interactions.

Experiments were conducted on a single-scenario 10-turbine configuration
and a full 540-scenario 5-turbine configuration. These experiments
demonstrate the implementation and evaluation of the formulation on
smaller instances and highlight the computational challenges of scaling
wake-aware optimization to the original 50-turbine problem.
